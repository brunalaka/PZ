OPTION 1: USABILITY ASSESSMENT
7 participants, unmoderated, Berlin to Hamburg seat selection flow

  P10's emotion is unresolved. Recommend checking recording at approximately 1:11.

- P3, Task 3: Participant said "the titles are blocked out" when viewing the seat category panel.
  Could be a device display issue or real text truncation in the design. Recommend checking at approximately 1:58.


TASK 1: Trip comparison (contextual)

All 7 scanned for price and journey time as their first two filters. Premium Zone acted as a
differentiator for 3 of 7 (P2, P4, P8): all three chose a trip specifically because it carried the
Premium Zone label, either as a tiebreaker when price was similar or as an active preference. P3
noticed the label but said "I don't know what Premium Zone means" and considered investigating before
booking. P1, P6, and P10 made their choices without mentioning it.

When the price gap is small, the Premium Zone badge does shift some people's trip choice. For roughly
half the group, it registered as noise at the results-list stage.


TASK 2: Seat selection (evaluative)

All 7 could identify that seat categories exist and read the legend. Icons and price differences read
clearly. Two participants (P2, P4) went looking for Premium seats on the lower deck first, then had to
switch to the upper deck. That is 2 of 7, below the threshold for a main finding, but a recurrent
navigation pattern worth noting.

P1's Task 2 navigation data is incomplete due to a prototype glitch mid-task. Their verbal commentary
on the legend was usable but their path through the map was not.


TASK 3: Premium Zone comprehension (evaluative, core)

DIFFICULTY: 3 of 7.

The specific benefits (privacy, comfortable seats, better wifi, priority boarding) were not apparent
without tapping into the seat category legend panel. P4 said explicitly "I can't find anything that
specifically describes the premium options" before eventually discovering the tappable panel. P1 didn't
find the info at all during this task, only discovering it when prompted by Task 6. P3 reached the panel
but could only read the description text due to a prototype issue; their comprehension data is still
usable.

What participants assumed without the info panel:
  - P1 and P2: physical seat reasoning (upper deck, better position, single vs double arrangement)
  - P4: guessed "more leg room"
  - P8: described it as "between economy and business" (directionally correct, no specific benefits)

P6 was the exception, having absorbed the value proposition from the results-page banner before reaching
the seat map, and could describe wifi and priority boarding without needing the panel.

Once participants found and read the legend panel, comprehension was immediate. The problem is
discoverability, not content.


TASK 4: Emotional reaction

Interested: P1, P2, P3, P4, P6 (5 of 7).
Curious: P8 (the small price gap triggered curiosity rather than straightforward interest).
Indifferent: P10.


TASK 5: Noticeability

EASE: 7 of 7. Every participant spotted the Premium options quickly, either on the results page or on
the seat map. P4 gave noticeability a 4 out of 5, but clarified their rating was about not realising the
legend was interactive, not about seeing the options. They were spotted immediately.


TASK 6: Understandability

DIFFICULTY: 3 of 7.

P1 said understanding required tapping first, and only rated it clearly after doing so. P4 gave it a 4
because they couldn't distinguish Premium from Premium Single: "the two pictures look very, very similar,
how is there more privacy? What aspects make it more private?" P3 found the wifi claim confusing: "how is
the wifi better, because the wifi is located at the front of the bus? That's a little weird." P2, P6, P8,
and P10 all rated it as easy or fairly clear.


TASK 7: What would make it easier

Two participants (P1, P4) independently suggested the legend panel needs an affordance to show it is
tappable: "knowing that you could click on something to get that information" (P1), "it should have said
click to view" (P4). One data point short of the 3-participant threshold, but directly tied to the core
discoverability issue above.

P8 raised the small price gap as confusing (one participant, left out of main findings). P3's wifi
explanation query is also a single voice and left out.


CONCLUSION

  Mentioned Premium Zone during trip comparison:
  4 of 7. P2, P4, and P8 used it as an active reason to choose a trip. P3 noticed it but said they
  didn't know what it meant.

  Discovered they could tap for more detail on each seat category:
  3 of 7. P4 found it mid-Task 3 after searching. P3 went back to look (reached the panel with a
  prototype limitation). P1 only found it when directly asked about understandability in Task 6. The
  other 4 did not discover the panel at all.

  Understood Premium Zone as more than just a seat category:
  3 of 7. P2, P4, and P6 arrived at the actual value proposition (wifi, comfort, priority boarding) as
  a zone-level experience. P8 understood it as a tier concept but not the specific benefits. P1, P3, and
  P10 did not get there.

  Most present emotion:
  Interested (5 of 7). The response was positive where it landed, but reaching it required finding the
  info panel, which most participants did not do unprompted.

The concept is legible and appealing once found. The path to finding it is broken for the majority of
participants.
