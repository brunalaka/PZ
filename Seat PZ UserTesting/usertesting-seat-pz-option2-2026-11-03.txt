OPTION 2: USABILITY ASSESSMENT
Test: 4f3fa688 | 8 sessions, P4 excluded (2-minute session, insufficient data) | 7 participants
(P1, P6, P7, P9, P10, P11, P12) | Unmoderated

Option 2 distinctive features from transcripts: results page "New Premium Zone" banner, seat category
pill/tag labels at the top of the seat map (each tappable for a description), seat types Classic / Front
/ Panorama / Premium / Premium Single (the single-occupancy premium seat is unique to Option 2).


FLAGS (check recordings before treating as complete)

- P9, Tasks 5 and 6 (most important): P9 rated noticing as "very easy" in Task 5, but in Task 6 said
  "I'm so sure it didn't do that the first time, I just thought I would tap the seat I wanted."
  The Task 5 response may refer to noticing the pill labels themselves, not realising they were tappable
  for detailed information. Worth checking to understand what P9 meant.

- P10, Task 4: No audio captured during the emotional reaction question. Emotion is unresolved.
  Marked as "not clearly stated" in the analysis.

- P7, Task 5: No audio captured. P7 is excluded from the noticeability count.

- P11, Tasks 6 and 7: No audio captured for either. P11's understanding rating and improvement
  suggestions are missing. Understanding was inferred from Task 2/3 context (position-only
  interpretation).


TASK 1: Trip comparison (results page)

EASE: 5 of 7 mentioned Premium Zone on first impression.

P11 read the banner and immediately filtered for premium-zone trips. P1 read the full expanded banner:
"First with comfort and privacy, a little more personal space." P12 explored the seat options directly
from the results card: "Oh wow, what's that? More privacy, more comfortable seats. Yeah, I like the idea
of that one." P9 noticed the banner and specifically wanted images alongside it. P7 casually noted
"premiums" while comparing trips. P6 and P10 did not call out Premium Zone during Task 1.


TASK 2: Seat selection (evaluative)

EASE: 4 of 7 were drawn to Premium Single spontaneously.

The "no one sitting next to me" framing resonated immediately. P12: "I like the idea of being in slight
isolation." P6: "definitely going for one of the singles if I'm travelling by myself." P9: "I really,
really like this one here." P10 also gravitated toward it. This landed clearly even before participants
had read the descriptions.

DIFFICULTY: 4 of 7. Tapping a seat vs tapping the category pill takes you to different places, and most
tapped the seat.

Option 2 has two interactive surfaces: the category pill at the top (shows description) and the
individual seats on the map (goes straight to seat selection). Multiple participants tapped seats rather
than pills and bypassed the benefit information entirely until prompted in Task 6.

P9: "When I actually tap on premium single it now brings up this. I'm so sure it didn't do that the
first time. I just thought I would tap the seat I wanted."
P1: "If I click on premium single, ah right, I didn't know if I clicked on it, I'd get information."
P7: "I really had to click on the premium bit to see it."

P12 was the only participant who had actively tapped the category pills from Task 1. P1, P9, and P7
only discovered the pill detail panel between Tasks 3 and 6.

DIFFICULTY: 3 of 7. Paid seating model was questioned.

P1: "I thought I'd already paid for premium zone in the other section, but I'm having to pay separate
for the seats, so I'm a bit confused." P9: "The fact that you still have to pay three euros for a
classic is kind of strange, I thought only the upgraded options would have an additional charge." P11:
"It's a bit cheeky asking people to pay extra on a bus."


TASK 3: Understanding of Premium options

P1 and P12, who had read the full banner or pill details, described the offer accurately (space, privacy,
wifi, priority boarding, single-occupancy distinction). P6 found the descriptions during Task 3: "Oh,
and there it says more privacy, more comfortable seats." P7 saw the photo card: "Oh damn, that's a nice
chair." P11 interpreted premium entirely as location and view: "You're sat at the front, basically
getting the best view possible kind of above the driver." No mention of benefits beyond position. P10
read the descriptions from the pills. P9 had explored from Task 2 and had the full picture.


TASK 4: Emotional reaction

Interested: P6, P7, P9 (3 of 7).
Curious: P12 ("They got me thinking, I like the idea. I do really like the idea of no one next to me").
Indifferent: P11 ("If I was on the front premium row, that would make sense. But row three not so much.
Too many rows marked as premium").
Confused: P1 ("I am a bit confused because I thought I'd already paid for premium").
Not clearly stated: P10.


TASKS 5/6: Noticeability and understandability

Noticeability (Task 5):
Easy: P11, P1, P12, P6, P9 all rated noticing as easy or very easy. No data for P7. P10 said "It was
that obvious to start with," which in context reads as "it wasn't immediately obvious," consistent with
their broader comments.

Understanding (Task 6):
P12 found it easy (had explored from Task 1). P7 rated it "very, very easy" (had found the photo card
in Task 3). P1 and P9 discovered the pill detail panel for the first time during Task 6 and their
understanding improved significantly afterwards. P10 said understanding was not initially clear. P6
understood the basics but remained sceptical about the wifi claim: "I'm not sure you can single out
seats to get worse wifi connections."


TASK 7: What could make the Premium options easier to understand

Three participants converged on the same solution: make it obvious that tapping the pill reveals
information.

P1: "Definitely have an eye link on each one. People know then to click on it and get the information,
because I thought there was no information at all."
P7: "They should let you tap on something that takes you straight to this screen. Because no one's
really gonna tap on these."
P9: "As soon as you select a seat it should automatically pop up that comparison and show you all the
different seating options, just to make sure you are definitely making an informed choice."

P11 felt no change was needed: "It is what it is. You're not getting extra. You're just getting that
seat." (Consistent with their position-only interpretation of premium.)

Additional suggestion from P9: reviews or ratings from real people who have travelled on that specific
seat type (single voice, not a pattern).


CONCLUSION

  Mentioned Premium Zone during trip comparison:
  5 of 7.

  Discovered they could see more details about each seat category:
  5 of 7 found the pill detail panel (P12 in Task 1, P6 and P7 in Task 3, P1 and P9 in Task 6). 2 of 7
  relied on banner context without accessing the pills (P10, P11).

  Understood Premium options as more than just a seat:
  5 of 7. Once the pill descriptions were found, most grasped the full offer. P11 described it only as
  location. P10 was partially clear.

  Most present emotion:
  Interested (3 of 7), with Curious adding a further 1 of 7.

The Premium Single concept generated the strongest spontaneous pull of any feature across participants.
The core discoverability issue is consistent with Options 1 and 3: the interactive affordance that
reveals the value proposition is not visible to most participants without prompting.
