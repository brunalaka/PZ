OPTION 3: USABILITY ASSESSMENT
Test: 74addba8 | 7 participants (P2, P5, P6, P7, P8, P9, P11) | Unmoderated

Option 3 distinctive features from transcripts: results page "New Premium Zone" banner, seat category
key at the top of the seat map with tappable photo cards per category, seat types Classic / Front /
Panorama / Premium.

Note: The Figma node linked for this test (4642:56980) resolves to a Contact form in the production
Checkout file, not a seat map frame. The prototype being tested was identified entirely from
transcripts.

No recording checks needed. One prototype rendering note was logged (P6, Task 5: "the page didn't kind
of load below where that key was") and treated as an artefact. P6's data is kept.


TASK 1: Trip comparison (results page)

EASE: 4 of 7 mentioned Premium Zone on first impression.

P5 read the banner immediately (initially confused by the term, then found the explanation). P7 was
"immediately interested" before comparing prices. P9 read the banner benefits while reviewing trips. P11
described the offer from the badge: "enjoy more comfortable journey, better wifi, that's included." P8,
P6, and P2 focused on price, time, and directness and said nothing about Premium Zone unprompted.


TASK 2: Seat selection (evaluative)

DIFFICULTY: 6 of 7. The category key at the top of the seat map was not perceived as interactive.

P7 was the only participant to tap a category card in Task 2 and see the photos: "I quite like the fact
that I can now see the seat categories." P6 noticed the key existed but did not tap it. The remaining
five navigated the seat map without engaging with the key at all. P11 discovered it in Task 6 and said
"I didn't realize you could click on it."

DIFFICULTY: 3 of 7. Premium Zone location on the seat map was hard to find.

P2: "I can't see a premium one. Am I looking straight past it or something?" P9 looked at the lower deck
and concluded "there's no premium seats on this." P7 (even in Task 1) asked "where's premium?" and
guessed upper deck front.

DIFFICULTY: 3 of 7. Paid seating model was questioned.

P8: "Having booked a ticket, I am expected to get a seat. I don't have to pay for a seat." P2: "Seems a
bit weird, already paid for a ticket." P6 expressed similar hesitation about value.


TASK 3: Premium Zone comprehension (evaluative, core)

Banner exposure reduced reliance on guesswork for 3 of 7. Participants who had read the results banner
or found the category cards (P5, P7, P11) described the offer accurately. Those without prior access
(P8, P6, P9) relied on assumptions: P8 expected "first class of the bus with leg room and power
charging," P9 guessed "at the back where it's more spacious, probably a panoramic roof," P6 was unsure
what a premium bus seat would even involve. P2 found the detail panel during this task while hunting for
where Premium seats were located.


TASK 4: Emotional reaction

Interested: P5, P11, P7 (3 of 7).
  P5: "definitely interested, premium wifi in this day and age."
  P11: "I'd say interested, based on comfort and location."
  P7: "I'm always interested in the bits that get you something nice."

Sceptical: P8, P9 (2 of 7).
  P8: "I feel sceptical because I don't understand what it entails, it just says premium seats, additional cost."
  P9: "sceptical because you don't know where it's gonna be and what the actual features are."

Confused: P6 (1 of 7).
Indifferent: P2 (1 of 7).


TASKS 5/6: Noticeability and understandability

Noticeability (Task 5):
Easy: P8, P5, P11, P7. P5 and P11 credited the results page banner directly.
Mixed (3 out of 5): P6.
Difficult: P2 (missed the upper/lower deck distinction), P9 ("I can't see the Premium Zone anywhere on
this page").

Understanding (Task 6):
The category detail panel was discovered mid-task by multiple participants, and that moment shifted their
assessment sharply.

P9: "I've clicked on the seat now... okay so now it's making it much clearer. If I could go back I would
change my previous answer to very easy."
P11: "I didn't realize you could click on it, but I thought I'd give it a go. Some more privacy, more
comfortable seats, better wifi, priority boarding. I think that's an absolute given."
P6: "I didn't see this... [after clicking] now that I've kind of seen that, I think it is easy to
understand."

Rated easy or improved to easy: P5, P7, P6, P9 (after finding the cards).
Mixed: P11 (gave 4/5 because the tapping wasn't obvious), P2 (gave 3/5).
Difficult throughout: P8. The only participant who never found the detail panel. "Not very easy, just cost."


TASK 7: What could make it easier

Six of seven participants converged on one theme: make it clear the category cards are tappable.

P11: "If there was an information logo next to the premium, I probably would've pressed it. Based on
looking at the pictures, 100% I would've chosen the premium zone seats."
P9: "You need to have something on each of those options to make it clearer that you need to click on it
to go to a different screen."
P2: "More of a tooltip style, a clearer way of indicating that you can see what you get for each seat."
P11 (reinforcing): "If there was a little 'i' next to the seat logo, I definitely would've clicked on it."

The unanimous signal: the content once revealed is persuasive. The affordance to reveal it is invisible.


CONCLUSION

  Mentioned Premium Zone during trip comparison:
  4 of 7. P5, P7, P9, and P11 (all via the results page banner).

  Discovered they could see more details about each seat category:
  1 of 7 found it spontaneously in Task 2 (P7). Five more found it across Tasks 3 to 6. One never
  found it (P8).

  Understood Premium Zone as more than just a seat category:
  6 of 7 (once the detail panel was found). P8 only ever saw the price add-on.

  Most present emotion:
  Interested (3 of 7).

The concept is legible and appealing once found. The path to finding it is invisible to most participants.
